# Mireli Driver: Kenya legal and driver experience review

**Reviewed:** 7 October 2026

**Purpose:** Assess the supplied “Msafiri Legal Compliance & Liability Mitigation Playbook” as source material and record the driver-app design direction.
**Status:** Product and engineering review, not a Kenyan legal opinion, compliance certificate, or permission to launch.

## My assessment of the playbook

It is a useful issue-spotting checklist: it correctly calls attention to driver status, passenger/vehicle safety, missed SGR connections, transport rules, personal data, money flows and criminal-safety controls. Its confidence goes too far, though. It calls contractual and technical controls a way to remain “fully shielded,” treats one driver classification as settled, cites at least one incorrect regulation number, and presents an unverified document list, tax treatment and 11% fee as if they prove legal compliance. Do not paste it into Mireli’s contracts, website or app as approved legal language.

The proposed sentence — “Mireli provides an electronic booking and matching technology service. Mireli is not a common carrier, transport operator, or employer” — may describe part of the product only if it matches Mireli’s actual conduct. A label in a contract cannot by itself settle the relationship or remove non-waivable duties. Before using it, Kenyan counsel should map who sets fares, accepts bookings, collects passenger money, assigns drivers, controls cancellations, owns/leases vehicles, contracts with passengers, handles complaints and carries insurance. Draft separate, consistent passenger, driver and vehicle-owner terms if those are the actual parties. A term should describe the service accurately and allocate responsibilities without claiming to erase statutory or court-recognized rights.

Permission to multi-hail, driver control over availability, clear refusal rules and transparent settlement are sensible product and operating choices. They support the stated commercial model only when the platform’s real behavior follows them. Vehicle ownership by a driver or Sacco does not, on its own, decide Mireli’s legal classification or remove the need to verify vehicle and operator requirements.

## Claims to correct or verify before adoption

| Playbook claim | Review | Mireli action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A disclaimer makes Mireli “fully shielded”; drivers are categorically not employees or carriers | Unsafe conclusion. The contract wording is one fact; actual arrangements, role and applicable law matter. The referenced UK Uber litigation is not a Kenyan ruling. | Ask Kenyan transport and employment counsel to assess Mireli’s real booking, payment, dispatch and support model. Avoid categorical claims in product copy. |
| The 2022 NTSA rules impose an 18% cap, so an 11% commission guarantees compliance | The original Legal Notice 120 of 2022 included an 18% cap. A High Court judgment reported on 1 September 2026 has since been summarized as suspending enforcement of that cap and some data-retention requirements while the government revisits the rules. The official Kenya Law judgment URL was discoverable but returned 403 in this review; I could not verify appeal, stay or subsequent legislative status. | Do not state that 11% is “legally compliant” because it is below 18%. Confirm the current order, appeal/stay status, replacement rules and Mireli’s actual regulatory category with counsel and NTSA before pricing or launch. |
| SOS/panic button is required by “Regulation 12” | In the published 2022 Legal Notice 120, regulation 12 concerns licence validity and renewal. The panic-button/24-hour response-centre wording appears under regulation 11(h). Later amendments and the 2026 judgment’s effect must be checked. | Correct the citation in any draft. Do not show an in-app SOS button as operational until it reaches a staffed response process, is tested end to end and has escalation/incident procedures. |
| TNC rules are “Gazette Notice No. 115” | The instrument located is **Legal Notice No. 120 of 2022**, not Gazette Notice 115. | Use the exact instrument citation only after counsel confirms it applies and remains in force as amended or affected by court orders. |
| Seven named uploads are mandatory by law, with fixed validity periods | Driver requirements vary by activity, vehicle, route, licensing category and current NTSA/insurer rules. Uber and Bolt publish different platform-specific checklists; those are useful operational references, not a universal statute. | Keep document requirements in a server-controlled, dated catalogue. For each item record legal/contractual basis, vehicle scope, issuer, expiry rules, review owner and retention period. Do not reject an applicant on an unverified hard-coded checklist. |
| Private third-party cover is always inadequate; a specific PSV policy and fixed KSh 250,000 micro-cover are mandatory | Commercial passenger-use cover needs written confirmation from a licensed insurer for Mireli’s actual service, vehicle type and passenger capacity. The named benefit/fee is a proposed product, not proof of statutory insurance or a binding insurance partnership. | Obtain policy wording, insurer confirmation, claim route, exclusions, premium/tax treatment and written partner agreement before advertising coverage or dispatching. Expiry blocks must be backed by verified policy data and an appeal path. |
| Missed-train disclaimer removes consequential-loss liability | A disclaimer does not validate an inaccurate ETA or automatically remove consumer, contract or negligence exposure. The playbook’s boarding/security cutoffs and ferry buffers need current operator evidence. | Publish realistic pickup windows, uncertainty, passenger readiness rules, disruption contacts and a clear refund/change policy. Do not promise a train connection or rely on a force-majeure paragraph as the sole mitigation. |
| Separate consent checkbox is required for every SMS, GPS and payment operation | Kenya’s Data Protection Act recognizes several lawful bases, including consent and processing necessary for a contract or legal obligation. Consent must be specific and freely given where used; a blanket checkbox is not a substitute for a lawful-basis and purpose assessment. | Map each data field and event to purpose, lawful basis, notice, recipient, retention and rights. Ask separately for optional uses. Request Android location permission only when the related driver feature needs it. |
| Location, identity and trip logs may simply be kept for three years | The Act requires data minimization, purpose limitation and security; high-risk processing may require a DPIA. A fixed retention rule should not be copied without validating the 2026 ruling and data-protection obligations. | Complete a DPIA before high-risk identity, screening or location processing; set purpose-specific retention/deletion; restrict staff access; protect documents in private storage; log reviewer access; use processor contracts and breach procedures. |
| Only the 11% commission is Mireli’s taxable turnover | This depends on the actual principal/agent and payment structure, who contracts for transport, who receives and controls money, and applicable income/VAT/eTIMS rules. A spreadsheet split does not decide tax treatment. | Have a Kenyan tax adviser document the accounting model, VAT/eTIMS obligations, withholding, reconciliations, refunds and payout treatment with KRA/provider evidence. Treat any payment provider as a regulated integration, not a tax conclusion. |
| OTP, boarding PIN, audit log and Daraja idempotency “solve” safety/fraud | These controls reduce specific risks but do not establish identity, insurer cover, passenger protection, lawful retention, payment settlement or a live emergency response. | Keep server-side authorization, replay protection, reconciliation, safety workflows and operational ownership. Test them with realistic failures before commercial trips. |

The Data Protection Commissioner’s public guidance explicitly lists transport services, including online passenger-hailing apps, among categories not exempt from mandatory controller/processor registration. Mireli should verify its role(s), register where required and keep its registration current. The Act’s high-risk DPIA trigger and the Data Protection (General) Regulations’ examples should be assessed against onboarding identity documents, background checks, precise location and trip monitoring. See the official [ODPC FAQs](https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/), [Data Protection Act](https://kenyalaw.org/kl/fileadmin/pdfdownloads/Acts/2019/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf), and [Data Protection (General) Regulations](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2021/263/eng%402022-01-14/source).

- [Traffic Act (Cap. 403)](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1953/39/eng%402022-12-31/source.pdf) — road and PSV provisions must be checked against Mireli’s vehicle/service model.

This review intentionally does not certify the company’s licensing, driver status, insurance, tax or privacy compliance. The pasted playbook is about “Msafiri”; its claims that particular code and admin features are already implemented are not evidence that they exist in Mireli Driver or Mireli Web.

## Driver-app design direction

Use Uber/Bolt as references for interaction patterns, not as visual assets, exact screen copies or legal proof. Their official Kenya onboarding pages emphasize a short registration sequence, phone/contact readiness, a document checklist, activation/review and a dedicated driver support path. Uber lists ID, PSV driving licence, screening, certification and profile photo; Bolt lists identity, licence, police clearance, photo and vehicle evidence. The differences reinforce that Mireli needs its own verified requirements.

Mireli should make the first useful action obvious and be specific to the SGR connection:

1. Put phone sign-in at the top of the first screen, with the service state directly below it. A status retry must say that it does not send an SMS.
2. Keep new-application intake separate from sign-in for existing drivers. The API should return separate `phoneSignInOpen` and `registrationOpen` flags.
3. After verification, show one clear application progress state: profile, vehicle, required documents, review, approval. Explain a rejection or expiry in plain language and allow secure replacement.
4. Once approved, make today’s assignment, pickup readiness and safe passenger verification the primary screen. Show actual earnings only from reconciled server records. Keep support visible and distinguish routine cases from emergencies.
5. Preserve Mireli’s own teal/navy brand, compact iOS-like surfaces, large touch targets and explicit light/dark choice. Do not invent live demand, map tracking, earnings, regulatory badges, approval or support coverage.

The Android sign-in screen has been adjusted in this branch to put the phone form first, separate service status from the OTP action, distinguish paused applications from phone sign-in, and move the promotional explanation below the useful controls. The signed-in assignment, onboarding, earnings and support areas remain server-backed. This local screen does not bring the missing production API online.

## Production and Play release gates

The app is **not production-ready or ready for Play submission** today. The pilot’s production hostname returned a passenger-site 404 for the driver status endpoint on 7 October 2026; consequently the app cannot request a real OTP or create a live application there. Do not re-enable “Check eligibility” as if it sends an SMS. The actual sequence is a successful driver API status check, an explicit “Continue with phone” tap, a server-accepted OTP request and delivered SMS, then verification. Eligibility follows authenticated server-side document, vehicle and compliance review.

| Gate | Evidence required before release |
| --- | --- |
| Shared booking service | Mireli Web, dispatcher and Driver use one authoritative booking/departure/seat/charter store; a real booking is assigned once and visible only to the authorized driver. |
| Live API and authentication | Production HTTPS driver status and endpoints deployed; existing-driver sign-in works even when recruitment is closed; SMS provider sender/configuration, rate limits, challenge expiry, replay and abuse tests pass. |
| Onboarding/privacy | Applicable driver/vehicle requirements approved by counsel and operations; privacy notice and driver terms published and linked before collection; private object storage, malware/content screening, least-privilege review access, expiry checks, deletion requests and backup/restore proven. |
| Safety and insurance | Confirmed vehicle/driver cover; incident and breakdown escalation; staffed emergency response if required by the current rules; safety features tested end to end, not just shown in the UI. |
| Finance | Payment collection, refunds, commission, settlement, failed/duplicate callbacks and payout reconciliation verified with the actual provider and Kenyan tax/accounting advice. |
| Google Play | Current privacy policy and accurate Data safety answers; account deletion both in-app and through a public web route if accounts can be created; support/contact and content-rating declarations; production-signed Android App Bundle and Play App Signing; internal/closed testing and crash/ANR review. |
| Current Android policy | This project targets API 36, meeting the new-app/update target level published for submissions from 31 August 2026. Recheck at upload; target level alone does not certify policy compliance. |

Google Play currently requires target API 36 for new apps and updates from 31 August 2026, requires data disclosures/privacy handling, and requires in-app plus external account-deletion routes when users can create accounts. See the official [target API policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11926878?hl=en), [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311), and [account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en). The project targets API 36, but this pilot is debug-signed and is not an uploadable Play release.
